Inside the experiment

The biology

Acanthamoeba feeds on bacteria by phagocytosis. A Mimivirus particle, about 0.75 µm across with its fibers, is large enough to pass for food. In the phagosome, five capsid faces around one vertex, the stargate, fold open, and the genome enters the cytoplasm. A viral factory grows beside the nucleus and assembles hundreds of new particles.

Sputnik, a 50 nm virophage, cannot multiply in the amoeba alone. In this model it enters attached to the fibers of a giant virion and replicates in the giant virus’s factory. The giant virus then makes fewer particles, and more of them are abortive or misassembled. The amoeba bursts either way. In the related Cafeteria–CroV–mavirus system, the released virophages protect the next host cells, not the one that made them.

The physics

The cell is a lattice of about 4,000 tetrahedra solved with extended position-based dynamics (XPBD). Each element carries an edge constraint and a volume constraint, and the cortex near the membrane is stiffer than the cytoplasm. The solver runs fixed 1/120 s steps with six substeps and per-edge viscous damping. The underside holds the slide through adhesions that creep slowly and peel off under a hard pull. Pseudopods, the rounding before lysis, and the collapse after it are changes in rest length, not keyframes. The nucleus, factory, vacuoles, granules and virions store rest-space coordinates and ride the deformation by barycentric interpolation.

The rendering

Four WebGPU passes run each frame: a top-down height map for contact shadows, a thickness buffer from front- and back-face depth, the slide with everything inside the cell, and then the membrane. The membrane pass adds screen-space refraction, Beer–Lambert absorption, Schlick Fresnel, a GGX highlight and thin-edge transmission. The vertex shader skins the surface from the tetrahedra.

Scale

The amoeba is about 25 µm across; the slide grid is 10 µm, subdivided at 2 µm. Giant virions are drawn at 1.5× and virophages at about 4× true size. Yields and timings are approximate. Fluorescence colors are false color: DNA in blue, giant virus in green, virophage in magenta.
